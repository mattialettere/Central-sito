Family and location

Family safety, without constant tracking

Many family apps show everyone where everyone is, all the time. Central starts from a different idea: location is shared when there is a reason, with people you choose, and the person being followed knows it and can say no.

In short: with Central a family stays in touch through an encrypted chat and uses location only when it matters: during a trip, when someone leaves an agreed zone or in an SOS. The "I'm OK" check-in alerts you when a confirmation does not arrive. The person being followed always sees who follows them and can disconnect. It is a private, European alternative to continuous-location apps such as Life360.

Two ways to think about family safety

The most popular family location apps are built on continuous sharing: everyone in the circle sees where everyone else is on a map, at any moment. It is convenient, but it means everyone's location is collected all the time, and for a teenager or an elderly parent it can feel more like control than help.

Central takes the opposite approach: location is sent only for a specific event that someone chose. The rest of the time nobody knows where you are, not even Central.

The tools, situation by situation

"Let me know when you get there": Safe Trip

The person leaving picks a destination and up to 5 people to follow them. Those people see a single dot moving to the destination and get an alarm if the arrival does not happen by the expected time. When the trip ends, location sharing stops. Locations are encrypted and deleted from the server 24 hours later.

"Tell me you're OK": the check-in

For people who live alone or for an elderly parent: you agree on a time, every day or only on some days. One tap on "I'm OK" is enough; if the confirmation does not arrive, the followers get an alert. No location sharing is needed.

"Has she left school?": safe zones

An agreed zone such as home or school. Followers get an alert when the person leaves and returns, without seeing their movements for the rest of the day. A zone is only created or changed by agreement. The free plan includes one zone, Plus three, Premium unlimited.

"I need help": SOS

Emergency contacts receive location, battery level and, if the SOS starts from the app, a 30-second video. On iPhone it can also start with Siri, on Android from the Quick Settings tile. Full details on the SOS and emergencies page.

Following from a computer: the Guardian dashboard

Anyone following a relative can use the web dashboard to see alerts, check-ins, trips and zones on one screen, together with the encrypted chat. The live map in the dashboard requires Plus or Premium.

Consent comes first

  • Nobody can follow you unless you accept their invitation.
  • Your profile always shows who receives your alerts and who your emergency contacts are.
  • You can disconnect from anyone with one tap, without asking permission.
  • Safe zones are only created and changed by agreement.

With children it is still important to talk about it as a family: a safety tool works best when the person using it understands why.

How to share your location in an emergency

Whatever app you use, a few rules help:

  1. In immediate danger call 112 first: across the EU, the single emergency number also receives the phone's location where the service is available.
  2. Share your location with a few trusted people, chosen before you need them.
  3. Add what a map cannot tell: battery level, what is happening, whether you can talk.
  4. Agree in advance on what the person receiving the alert should do.

In Central the SOS gathers location and battery automatically and, from the app, a short video, so the person receiving it understands the situation right away.
