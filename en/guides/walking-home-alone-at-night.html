Getting home alone at night: be prepared

Most journeys home go perfectly well. A few simple habits make both the person travelling and the person waiting more relaxed.

Download for iPhone Download for Android

Updated on 24 November 2026

In short: before you go out, charge your phone and decide how you will get back. Before you set off, tell someone your route and when you expect to arrive. On the way, choose well-lit, busy streets and stay aware of your surroundings. When you arrive, confirm you're home. An app that shares your location only for the journey does the last two for you.

Before you go out

  • Battery: leave with a charged phone or a power bank. A dead phone can't ask for help.
  • The way back: decide in advance how you'll get home. Last bus or train, taxi, a lift with a friend. Save the number of a local taxi service.
  • Emergency contacts: check they are set up on your phone. See our guide to emergency contacts.

Before you set off

Tell one person: "leaving now, home around midnight". It is the simplest and most effective rule, because someone knows when to expect you. The limit is that you have to remember to text when you arrive too, and the person waiting can't tell whether a delay is normal.

Journey apps automate exactly this. With Central's Safe Trip you choose a destination and up to 5 people to follow you: they see your route until you arrive and get an alert if you don't arrive by the expected time. When you confirm your arrival, sharing stops. There is also a walk mode, with no destination.

The difference from continuous tracking matters: your location is shared only for that journey, with people you chose, and then it stops.

On the way

  • Choose well-lit, busy streets, even if they take longer.
  • Keep your phone in your pocket when you don't need it, and headphones low or just one earbud in: it helps you stay aware of what's going on.
  • On public transport, sit near the driver or in carriages with other people.
  • If something feels wrong, go into an open shop, a pharmacy or anywhere with people, and call someone.

If something happens

If you are in immediate danger, call the emergency number (112 in the EU). If you also want to alert the people you trust, an SOS is faster than typing a message: in Central you can start it even with the phone locked, with Siri ("Hey Siri, Central SOS") on iPhone or from the Quick Settings tile on Android, and it sends your location and battery to your emergency contacts. Our guide on what to say in an emergency explains what to tell emergency services.

When you arrive

Confirm that you've arrived, even if it's late. The person waiting would rather get a message at midnight than spend the night worrying. With a journey app, one tap confirms your arrival and the people following you see it straight away.
