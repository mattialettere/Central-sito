In short: before sharing your location, ask yourself who with, for how long and why. Whenever you can, pick a limited time instead of "always". Every now and then, check the list of people who can see where you are and remove anyone who no longer needs to: on iPhone, Android and WhatsApp it takes a few taps, and the person removed usually isn't notified.

Three questions before you share

  • Who with? A few trusted people, chosen one by one. In groups, every member sees your location, including people you barely know.
  • For how long? A journey, an evening, a hike. Sharing "forever" only makes sense with someone who really needs to know where you are at any moment, and that's rare.
  • Why? If the goal is to know you've arrived or that you're fine, an arrival alert or a check-in is often enough, without a map that is always on.

Choose a time limit, not "always"

The most common tools let you limit the time:

  • iPhone (Find My and Messages): Apple explains that you can share your location for an hour, until the end of the day, indefinitely or for a custom time period. Both phones need iOS 15 or later; some options require iOS 17.
  • Google Maps: when you start sharing your real-time location you choose how long; one option is "Until you turn this off", best avoided unless you need it. People you share with also see your battery level and whether your phone is charging.
  • WhatsApp: live location is shared for a period you choose and stops by itself when it ends. WhatsApp says it is end-to-end encrypted. After it stops, the chat keeps an image of your last location.

Sending your location once, as a pin in a chat, is different: it shows where you are at that moment and doesn't follow you as you move.

Check who can see you, and stop when needed

Over time the list grows: a friend for a trip, an ex-partner, a colleague. It's worth reviewing it now and then.

  • iPhone: in Find My, People tab, tap the name and stop sharing your location, or turn off Share My Location in the Me tab to stop for everyone. Apple notes that the person you stop sharing with won't be notified. Safety Check helps you quickly stop sharing your information, including your location.
  • Android: in Google Location Sharing settings you see the list of people and can stop sharing with each one or pause it for everyone. Google warns that removing Maps' location permission or using Incognito mode does not stop Location Sharing.
  • WhatsApp: in the privacy settings, under live location, you see the chats where you are sharing and can stop with one tap.

If you think someone is tracking you

If you are worried that someone can see your location without your consent, review the lists above and the apps installed on your phone. Apple publishes a Personal Safety User Guide with step-by-step instructions for iPhone. If you are in danger, call the emergency number (112 in the EU) or a local support line.

How Central does it

Central has no always-on map: your location is only shared for an event you choose.

  • Safe Trip: up to 5 people follow a journey until you arrive, then sharing stops. Locations are end-to-end encrypted and deleted from the server after 24 hours.
  • Safe zones: followers get an alert when the person leaves or returns to an agreed zone, without seeing any other movements.
  • SOS: your location and battery go to your emergency contacts only when you ask for help.

In your profile you always see who receives your alerts, and you can disconnect from anyone with one tap.
