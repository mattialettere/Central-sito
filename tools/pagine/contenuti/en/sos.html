In short: Central's SOS sends your emergency contacts your location, your battery level and, when you start it from the app, a 30-second video, all end-to-end encrypted. It can be triggered from the app, from Siri or Control Center on iPhone, and from the widget or the Quick Settings tile on Android. Central does not call emergency services: if you are in danger, always call 112 or your local emergency number.

What an SOS app is for

Smartphones already have built-in emergency calling: Emergency SOS on iPhone and Emergency SOS on Android. They call emergency services. Across the EU the single emergency number is 112, free from any phone.

An SOS app like Central does something else: it alerts people you trust, who know you, know where you live and can reach you or call for help on your behalf. The two complement each other; one does not replace the other.

What your contacts receive

  • Your location, if the phone can get a fix. If it cannot, the SOS still goes out, marked "location unavailable" instead of showing a wrong point on the map.
  • Your battery level, so they know how long you may stay reachable.
  • A 30-second video from the front camera, when the SOS starts from the app or the Android widget. You can cancel within the first 3 seconds.
  • A prompt to call 112.

The alert arrives as a message in the encrypted chat with each contact, with a notification. The video is encrypted with a key used only for that SOS, which travels inside the encrypted message.

How to trigger it

On iPhone

  • From the SOS button in the app, with the 30-second video.
  • With Siri: "Hey Siri, Central SOS". It also works with the phone locked; the app's shortcuts must be enabled in Siri settings.
  • From Control Center, after adding Central's SOS control.

From Siri and Control Center the SOS goes out immediately with location and battery, without video.

On Android

  • From the SOS button in the app or the home-screen widget, with the 30-second video.
  • From the SOS tile in Quick Settings (the shade you pull down from the top), which sends immediately with location and battery, without video.

What about the volume button?

Central does not use volume-button press sequences. On many Android phones the system never passes those keys to apps, so the trigger would be unreliable: quick activation uses the Quick Settings tile instead, which works on every device.

How many emergency contacts you can have

The free plan allows 2 emergency contacts, Plus up to 5 and Premium unlimited. Each contact is notified when you choose them and needs Central to receive the alert. Your profile always shows who receives your SOS, and you can change it at any time.

When there is no network

Without a connection no app can send anything. Central, however, never drops an SOS silently: it retries several times over a few minutes with growing intervals, and if the app is closed before it succeeds, it resends when the app reopens. If the video slows things down, later attempts go out without it, because getting the alert and location through matters more.

How to prepare before you need it

  1. Choose your emergency contacts and tell them: they should know what to do if they receive an SOS.
  2. Grant location permission, otherwise the SOS goes out without coordinates.
  3. On iPhone, try "Hey Siri, Central SOS" with a contact you warned in advance; on Android, add the SOS tile to Quick Settings.
  4. Remember that in immediate danger your first call is to 112.

Quick questions

Does Central's SOS call 112?

No. It alerts your emergency contacts and prompts them to call 112. To call emergency services, use your phone or the system's Emergency SOS.

Who receives my location?

Only the emergency contacts you chose and, if you belong to a company that uses Central and you accepted, its supervisors. Nobody else.

Does SOS work when the app is closed?

Yes, from the Quick Settings tile on Android and from Siri or Control Center on iPhone. Location permission is needed to include coordinates.
